# LIST — HORROR RULES

## Purpose

This document protects LIST from drifting into generic horror design.

## Core Rules

1. Curiosity is the primary emotional driver.
2. Horror should emerge from uncertainty and discovery.
3. Avoid constant jumpscares.
4. Avoid combat as a primary system.
5. Do not turn LIST into a conventional FPS.
6. First-person presence must remain important.
7. Cinematics should not replace gameplay.
8. The world should feel ordinary before anomalies become apparent.
9. Environmental storytelling should carry meaningful information.
10. Horror mechanics must have consistent underlying rules.
11. Do not reveal the full truth too early.
12. Earlier details should gain new meaning after revelations.

## Observation / SEEN Concept

A major concept involves a threat or phenomenon related to being seen/observed.

Previously established story language includes:

- **SEEN**
- **DON'T LOOK AT IT**
- **Jangan biarkan dia melihat**

However, the exact mechanical rule is **PROVISIONAL**.

Do not automatically implement a generic "don't look at the monster" mechanic. The eventual rule must be justified by LIST's world and story.

## Knowledge Is Part of the Horror

The player may discover information that changes the meaning of something previously seen.

The horror should sometimes come from realizing that an ordinary object, person, recording, or event had a different meaning all along.

## Unresolved

- Exact nature of SEEN
- Trigger conditions
- Consequences
- Whether the phenomenon is supernatural/physical/psychological/ambiguous
- Exact limits of the threat
